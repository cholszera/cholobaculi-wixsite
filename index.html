Science, Culture & Ethics in Information Technology

Epistemological dialectics, sociological dimensions, and ethical frameworks in computing.

Theoretical & Ethical Core
Foundational Inquiry
Open Slide Deck (PDF)

This module examined the symbiotic relationship between scientific discovery and technological innovation. It explored how human culture both inspires and is radically reshaped by technological tools, and introduced the essential moral codes that govern professional software developers and systems administrators.

Science vs. Technology

Science is the systematic study of the physical universe through empirical observation (the pursuit of knowing why). Technology (from Greek 'technologia') is the practical application of scientific knowledge to create artifacts that solve human problems (the pursuit of knowing how).

Science drives technology with knowledge; technology provides science with instruments and new experimental frontiers.

The 8 Elements of Culture

Culture is the complex whole of social norms, beliefs, and practices transmitted across generations (Taylor, Linton, Beattee). The 8 core pillars include:

1. Religion 2. Daily Life 3. Economy 4. Government 5. Arts 6. History 7. Social Groups 8. Language

5 Ways Tech Reshaped Work

  • Mobility: Untethered computing and mobile enterprise workstations.
  • The Cloud: Centralized data persistence and distributed collaboration.
  • VR & AR Innovation: Spatial computing, remote maintenance, and digital modeling.
  • Customer Data: Predictive behavioral analytics and personalized CRM pipelines.
  • Social Impact: Global awareness, digital advocacy, and decentralized labor.

Critical IT Ethical Dilemmas

  • Workplace Monitoring: Balancing corporate asset management against employee privacy rights.
  • Copyright Piracy: Illegal distribution of proprietary code, media, and digital assets.
  • Plagiarism & Integrity: Copying code repositories and academic materials without attribution.
  • Telemetry & Spyware: Unauthorized cookies tracking user behavior without informed consent.
  • Identity Theft: Malicious breaches exploiting unpatched vulnerabilities for financial gain.

Module 4 • Week 5

The Digital Divide: Dimensions, Consequences & Bridging Initiatives

Structural inequality in modern ICT, compound socioeconomic exclusion, and the UN SDG framework.

Social Justice & Digital Equity
UN SDG Alignment
Open Slide Deck (PDF)

The digital divide represents one of the most critical challenges of our century: the widening chasm between demographics and regions that have access to modern information and communications technologies and those that do not. In a world where essential services, banking, healthcare, and employment are digitized, lack of access compounds poverty into systemic disenfranchisement.

The 3 Levels of Digital Divide

  • 1. Access Divide (First-Level): Disparities in physical access to computers, mobile devices, and reliable internet connectivity.
  • 2. Skills Divide (Second-Level): Disparities in technical literacy, operational know-how, and the cognitive capability to evaluate digital resources.
  • 3. Usage Divide (Third-Level): Disparities in the economic, professional, and educational return individuals derive from digital tools.

The Urban-Rural Divide Factor

Identified as the single biggest factor in the digital divide. Commercial telecom operators systematically under-invest in remote, rural, and island regions due to lower return on investment, depriving agricultural and indigenous communities of the foundational fiber and cellular backbones available in metropolitan hubs.

Compound Societal Consequences

  • Educational Disadvantage: Students without computers fall permanently behind in STEM and higher learning.
  • Economic Exclusion: Inability to participate in the global digital remote labor market and e-commerce ecosystems.
  • Healthcare Disparities: Remote communities unable to access telemedicine during medical crises.
  • Civic Disenfranchisement: Isolation from online e-governance, social subsidies, and public consultations.

Strategic Bridging Initiatives

  • UN SDGs: Global mobilization under the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals to eradicate digital discrimination.
  • Universal Infrastructure: Government-subsidized municipal fiber backbones and free community Wi-Fi hubs.
  • Affordable Access: Subsidized student hardware and low-cost broadband data tariffs.
  • Inclusion for Disabilities: Mandating accessible screen readers, closed-captioning, and ergonomic software.
Module 5 • Weeks 5 & 6

Industry 4.0: Cyber-Physical Systems & The 9 Pillars

The Fourth Industrial Revolution, smart factories, autonomous operations, and Philippine enterprise applications.

Industrial Transformation
Smart Manufacturing
Open Slide Deck (PDF)

Industry 4.0 represents the complete digital transformation of industrial production through the synthesis of physical machinery, advanced computational networks, and cognitive data analytics. Powered by Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) and the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), factories evolve from automated islands into interoperable, self-optimizing ecosystems.

The 4 Core Benefits of Industry 4.0

  • Intelligent Products: Connected telemetry-emitting assets that monitor their own health and usage (Tesla OTA updates, Apple Watch).
  • Intelligent Factories (Factory 4.0): Self-organizing shop floors dynamically optimizing production schedules (Siemens Amberg).
  • Intelligent Assets: Real-time vibration, thermal, and sensor analytics enabling predictive maintenance before breakdowns occur.
  • Empowered Workers: Field technicians enhanced by augmented reality headsets (Microsoft HoloLens, Boeing AR glasses).

Enterprise Opportunities

  • Productivity Surge: Continuous 24/7 autonomous production with minimal error rates.
  • Agile Customization: Mass personalization produced at the cost efficiency of bulk manufacturing.
  • Resilient Supply Chains: Real-time inventory synchronization across global logistics networks.
  • Sustainable Green Manufacturing: Substantial energy conservation through automated optimization.

Systemic Adoption Challenges

  • Capital Expenditure: Exorbitant upfront capital costs to refit brownfield manufacturing plants.
  • Skills Shortage: Severe global scarcity of data engineers, roboticists, and cybersecurity analysts.
  • Cyber-Physical Attack Surfaces: Vulnerability of interconnected industrial control systems (SCADA) to cyber warfare.
  • Workforce Displacement: Automation of repetitive manual labor demanding massive national re-skilling programs.

Philippine Enterprise Case Studies

Philippine industry actively deploys Industry 4.0 technologies: Coca-Cola Beverages PH (Robotic assembly), Meralco (IIoT smart grid monitoring), SM Supermalls (Big Data foot-traffic analytics), GCash (AI credit scoring), PLDT (Cloud infrastructure), DOST (Additive 3D manufacturing), and BDO (Zero-trust financial cybersecurity).

Personal Synthesis • Midterms

Personal Growth & Professional Accountability

Living in the IT Era (GEITE01X) • Mentored by Dr. Earl Marquez Albina, LPT, DIT

School of IT • NU MOA • INF257

When I first started this semester in Living in the IT Era, I assumed it would be another standard lecture course where we just review computer history and browse through PowerPoint slides. As a BSIT student majoring in Mobile and Web Applications, my focus has almost always been on the practical mechanics: writing clean syntax, structuring databases, and making responsive web layouts.

Dr. Earl Marquez Albina’s classes completely shifted how I view my chosen career. Right from our first orientation, doc Earl challenged us with his guiding principle: “It is better to get an honest zero, than a cheated one hundred.” In programming, shortcuts are everywhere—you can easily copy code or lean on automated generators without truly grasping how things function. But doc Earl taught us that real engineering ability cannot be faked. Facing an error, debugging through frustration, and building with personal integrity is where real growth takes place.

Our lessons on Science, Culture, and Ethics in IT made me understand that software is never neutral. Every app we deploy interacts directly with human behavior, user privacy, and social trust. When we studied the digital divide, it hit me that fast internet and modern devices are privileges many families in remote areas still lack. For me as an upcoming developer, accessibility isn't just a design option—it's an ethical obligation.

Building this e-portfolio helped me step back and see how every concept connects. I'm moving forward with a deeper respect for honest craftsmanship, personal accountability, and the human side of information technology.

Business Intelligence & Analytics

Using accurate information to guide sound organizational decisions.

Slide Deck Part 1 (PDF) ↗ Slide Deck Part 2 (PDF) ↗

Business Intelligence (BI) helps organizations turn operational data into actionable insights for decision-making. The lessons focused on how data moves from collection to presentation, why information quality matters, and how analytics supports both everyday management and long-term planning.

1. The 5 Stages of Business Intelligence

  • Sourcing: Gathering raw data from internal databases, point-of-sale systems, and user interactions.
  • Data Analysis: Converting disparate data streams into consistent, query-ready formats through ETL pipelines.
  • Situational Awareness: Filtering noise to identify what is actually happening in the organization right now.
  • Risk Analysis: Modeling potential outcomes and evaluating trade-offs before committing resources.
  • Decision Support: Delivering clear dashboards and visualizations so stakeholders can act with confidence.

2. Information Quality: Why Good Data Matters

A decision is only as good as the information behind it. Low-quality data leads to misallocated budgets, missed trends, and flawed policies. High-quality data requires:

  • Accuracy: Verifiable correctness without data entry errors or bias.
  • Completeness: Full dataset context without missing fields or selective omissions.
  • Timeliness: Up-to-date information delivered when decisions need to be made.
  • Consistency: Uniform definitions and standards across different departments and tables.
Lesson 11

Communication Technology

Tools for collaboration, social networks, and responsible information habits.

Open Slide Deck (PDF) ↗

Communication technology shapes how we share knowledge, connect with peers, and conduct business. This module explored modern communication tools, social media categories, the real harms of unchecked misinformation, and a practical 7-step checklist before sharing content online.

1. Core Communication Tools

Modern workplaces combine multiple synchronous and asynchronous channels:

  • Messaging Apps: Quick team coordination via Slack, Microsoft Teams, and WhatsApp.
  • Video Conferencing: Remote face-to-face meetings via Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams.
  • Collaborative Workspaces: Shared documentation in Notion, Google Workspace, and Microsoft 365.
  • Social Media: Public-facing marketing, community building, and brand engagement.

2. Categories of Social Media Platforms

  • Social Networks: Building relationship graphs (Facebook, LinkedIn).
  • Media Sharing: Visual and audio content (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Spotify).
  • Discussion Forums: Topic-focused community dialogue (Reddit, Quora).
  • Microblogging: Short-form real-time commentary (X / Twitter, Threads).

3. Information Harms

  • Misinformation: False information shared without harmful intent (honest errors).
  • Disinformation: Deliberately fabricated falsehoods engineered to deceive.
  • Malinformation: Genuine information shared maliciously to cause personal harm.
  • Manipulated Content: Real images or audio altered using editing tools or deepfakes.

4. 7-Step Verification Checklist Before Sharing

  1. Read the full article beyond the sensational headline.
  2. Verify the publisher, author credentials, and domain.
  3. Check the original publication date and timeline.
  4. Examine cited sources, primary links, and evidence.
  5. Recognize personal confirmation bias.
  6. Cross-check against established independent newsrooms.
  7. Pause and refrain from forwarding unverified claims.
Lesson 12

Intellectual Property & Data Privacy

Protecting creative work and respecting personal information under RA 10173.

IP & Privacy (PDF) ↗ Updated Deck (PDF) ↗

Intellectual property protects inventions, designs, brands, and original software code. Data privacy protects individuals in the processing of their personal information. Both legal pillars are foundational for BSIT developers building software for the public.

Common forms of intellectual property protection in the Philippines
Protection What it covers Key requirements General protection period
Patent A technical solution, such as an apparatus, process, or product. New, inventive step, and industrially applicable. 20 years from filing date (non-renewable).
Utility model New technical solutions with simpler registration procedures. New and industrially applicable; no inventive step required. 7 years from filing date (non-renewable).
Industrial design The ornamental appearance, lines, patterns, or colors of an article. New or original; not dictated solely by technical utility. 5 years from filing, renewable up to two 5-year periods.
Trademark Distinctive words, logos, emblems, or sounds distinguishing goods. A distinctive, registrable, non-generic sign or symbol. 10 years from registration, renewable indefinitely every 10 years.
Copyright Original literary and artistic works, including software and source code. Arises automatically upon creation of the original work. Lifetime of the author plus 50 years after death.

The 8 Statutory Data Subject Rights (RA 10173)

Under the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012, individuals hold eight core rights:

  • 1. Right to be Informed: Knowing whether personal data is being processed, why, and by whom.
  • 2. Right to Access: Reasonable access to personal data held by data controllers.
  • 3. Right to Object: Withholding consent for automated processing or direct marketing.
  • 4. Right to Erasure or Blocking: Requesting removal of incomplete, outdated, or unlawfully obtained data.
  • 5. Right to Rectification: Disputing and correcting inaccurate personal records.
  • 6. Right to Data Portability: Obtaining a copy of electronic data in a structured, usable format.
  • 7. Right to File a Complaint: Lodging grievances directly with the National Privacy Commission (NPC).
  • 8. Right to Damages: Seeking indemnification for damages sustained due to privacy violations.

Conditions & Developer Responsibilities

These statutory rights are not absolute; they operate alongside legitimate research exceptions, criminal investigations, and tax compliance mandates. For software developers, the key lesson is adopting Privacy by Design: minimizing the data collected, storing only what is strictly required, implementing end-to-end encryption, and providing clear user account management controls.

Written Self-Reflection • Finals

What I Learned and How I Will Apply It

Living in the IT Era (GEITE01X) • Mentored by Dr. Earl Marquez Albina, LPT, DIT

School of IT • NU MOA • INF257

Reaching the end of Living in the IT Era feels like stepping back and seeing the entire picture come into focus. Moving into our Finals modules, our discussions tackled the practical realities of modern computing: Business Intelligence, data analytics, communication networks, intellectual property, and data privacy.

Lessons 9 and 10 on Business Intelligence completely changed how I think about databases. Before, I saw databases simply as storage buckets for user inputs. But understanding the ETL pipeline—gathering, cleaning, storing, analyzing, and presenting—taught me that data is only valuable when it is clean and accurate. Having more data doesn't guarantee better choices; if your information is flawed, automated systems only magnify those mistakes at a larger scale.

The modules on Communication Technology and Information Harms were equally grounded in reality. Working through the 7-step verification checklist has become an everyday habit for me. Now, before I share a post or cite a technical resource, I check the author, date, and context rather than taking things at face value.

Most importantly, Lesson 12 on Intellectual Property and the Philippine Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) reinforced my legal and ethical duties as a software developer. Understanding patents, trademarks, and copyright showed me that respecting others' intellectual work is non-negotiable. Furthermore, studying the 8 statutory Data Subject Rights proved that user privacy isn't an afterthought you slap on before launching—it must be baked into the core architecture of your system from day one.

I want to express my sincere appreciation to Dr. Earl Marquez Albina for his dedicated guidance throughout this trimester. Because of his mentorship, I step forward not just with academic grades, but with a grounded moral compass that will guide every platform I build.
